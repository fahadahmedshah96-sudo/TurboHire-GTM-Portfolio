# Audience, ICP, industries, and use cases

## Public-safe audience description

Enterprise organizations with recurring or distributed hiring needs, multiple concurrent roles, complex stakeholder coordination, or a high volume of applicants. Avoid publishing a precise company-size threshold unless it appears in an approved public brief.

## Buying and working audiences

- CHRO and senior HR leaders: workforce availability, consistency, control, and business readiness.
- Head of Talent Acquisition: hiring throughput, process performance, recruiter capacity, and visibility.
- Recruiters: candidate progression, follow-ups, prioritization, and exceptions.
- Hiring managers and interviewers: timely review, feedback, and approvals.
- HR technology, IT, and operations stakeholders: system fit, configuration, governance, integrations, and regional requirements.

Match the message to the role. Do not assume every reader owns the same problem or decision.

## Industry examples

The working industry set includes Automotive, BFSI, BPO/KPO, FMCG and Consumer Goods, GCC, Healthcare, Hospitality, ITES, Logistics and Supply Chain, Manufacturing, QSR, and Retail.

These are potential contexts, not proof that every industry or job type has the same requirements. Use a specific scenario and verify any industry-specific claim.

## Hiring scenarios

- Field Sales
- Field Operations
- Employee Referral
- Walk-In
- Campus and early talent
- Lateral hiring
- Blue- and grey-collar hiring
- Leadership hiring

Use frontline positioning when the role directly supports operations, customer delivery, or revenue. Do not force the category onto specialist or low-volume hiring without a clear reason.

## Scenario framing examples

| Scenario | Operating condition to explore |
| --- | --- |
| Field Sales | Hiring coverage across territories and revenue-facing teams. |
| Field Operations | Hiring and coordination across distributed locations. |
| Referral | Employee participation and candidate progression after referral. |
| Walk-In | A concentrated candidate flow that must be handled during a time-bounded event. |
| Campus | Structured movement across large entry-level cohorts and recruiting stages. |
| Manufacturing | Plant, shift, technical, and multi-site staffing needs. |
| Retail and QSR | New-location demand, ongoing replacement hiring, and store readiness. |
| Logistics | Hiring across hubs, facilities, and distributed operations. |

Treat these as prompts for research and messaging, not verified customer outcomes. Build each example from current evidence or label it clearly as illustrative.

## Regional discipline

Separate India, US, SEA, and global messaging. Candidate channels, compliance, identity checks, product maturity, labor terminology, and integrations may vary by market. Use the relevant regional source for each claim. In US-facing material, use terms such as “hourly,” “start,” “offer-to-start,” and “preboarding” when they fit. Do not move country-specific capabilities into a global statement.
