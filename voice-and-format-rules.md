# Voice, terminology, and format rules

## Shared voice

Write like an experienced operator explaining how a hiring process works. Be calm, clear, practical, and specific. Explain the cause, operating mechanism, trade-off, and consequence. Use concrete detail without turning the explanation into a list of symptoms.

Prefer measured language and natural sentence rhythm. Keep the language useful to an HR or business reader who may not know the internal product vocabulary.

## Avoid

- Unsupported superlatives, AI hype, fear, generic futurism, and claims of guaranteed outcomes.
- Rhetorical questions, excessive punchlines, repeated one-line fragments, and generic transitions.
- Em dashes and forced “not X but Y” contrasts.
- Stacked lists of problems or activities where a causal explanation is clearer.
- Generic claims that every ATS is fragmented or that AI automatically improves hiring.
- Overusing “AI-powered” without saying what the technology does.

## Terminology

- Write **SuperAgent** as one word with capital S and A.
- Write **Recruiter Copilot** in external copy. It informs and analyzes; it does not execute configured actions.
- Use **process**, not **journey**, in pre-onboarding and related material.
- Use **ITES**, not “IT,” when referring to that industry category.
- Define “frontline” broadly and accurately; do not equate it with hourly or blue-collar hiring alone.
- Preserve the distinction between high-volume hiring (scale) and frontline hiring (workforce and operating context).
- Keep “self-driving hiring” out of category headlines unless a current, approved campaign brief specifically requires it.

## Format-specific rules

### Sales and marketing decks

- Start with audience, region, decision, and evidence available.
- Build a cause-to-system-to-outcome narrative, not a feature parade.
- Give each slide one job and choose the visual form that explains it.
- Separate released, beta, pilot, and planned capabilities.
- Use Archivo for headings, Manrope for supporting text, and #6E3EFE as the primary accent where appropriate.

### eBooks and industry reports

- Build one sustained argument and make every chapter advance it.
- Use a small number of relevant, sourced facts. Record source, year, geography, definition, and limitations.
- Explain mechanisms and operating implications; avoid stitching together blog-style summaries.
- Distinguish primary research, company interpretation, illustrative examples, and product claims.

### Blogs

- Preserve the searcher's actual intent; use positioning only where it fits the topic.
- Explain the operating mechanism, rather than compiling generic headings from search results.
- Include a practical, clearly labeled example and a dedicated TurboHire section in substantial blogs.
- Retain a TurboHire comparison table when relevant. Metrics and FAQs are optional.
- Do not invent sources, capabilities, integrations, customer results, or statistics.

### Social posts and newsletters

- Lead with a specific insight or evidence-backed operating observation.
- Keep the tone calm and useful. Explain why the development matters to the intended audience.
- Attribute external perspectives and data. Do not present a company interpretation as a measured fact.
- Use a soft, contextual call to action.

## Brand basics

- Primary accent: `#6E3EFE`.
- Slide and editorial type: Archivo headings; Manrope subheads/body when available.
- Use the exact approved TurboHire logo asset when a branded visual requires it. Do not redraw or approximate the mark.
